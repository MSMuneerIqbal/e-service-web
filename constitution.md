# Website Constitution

## Purpose

This document is the governing constitution for the website project. It defines the principles, standards, constraints, decision-making rules, and quality expectations that every design, content, UX, SEO, and implementation decision must follow.

The website represents a professional **Amazon and eBay marketplace management and growth business**. Its job is not merely to describe services. Its primary purpose is to establish trust, communicate expertise, demonstrate a complete operational capability, and convert qualified marketplace sellers into consultation leads.

This constitution must be treated as the source of truth when later specifications, design decisions, component choices, copywriting, and implementation plans are created.

---

# 1. Product Vision

## 1.1 Core Positioning

The business must be positioned as a **marketplace growth and management partner**, not as a generic digital marketing agency.

Primary positioning:

> **We manage, optimize, and grow Amazon and eBay stores.**

The website should communicate that the company can support a seller across the marketplace lifecycle:

**Research → Product Selection → Listing → Optimization → Store Operations → Orders → Customer Support → Growth**

## 1.2 Primary Business Goal

The primary conversion goal is:

> **Generate qualified leads for marketplace management and growth services.**

Secondary goals are:

- Establish credibility.
- Explain the service offering.
- Demonstrate operational expertise.
- Educate potential clients.
- Build organic search visibility.
- Create a strong professional brand.

## 1.3 Target Audience

Primary audiences include:

- New Amazon sellers.
- New eBay sellers.
- Existing sellers with weak sales.
- Sellers struggling with product selection.
- Sellers needing listing optimization.
- Sellers who need day-to-day marketplace management.
- Sellers who want customer support and order handling.
- Sellers looking for a long-term marketplace operations partner.

The audience is generally business-oriented. They care about revenue, operational reliability, marketplace compliance, conversion, efficiency, and return on investment.

## 1.4 Audience Questions the Website Must Answer

Within the first few seconds, a visitor should understand:

1. What does this company do?
2. Which marketplaces does it support?
3. What problems can it solve?
4. Why should I trust it?
5. What should I do next?

If a section does not help answer one of these questions or support the conversion journey, its necessity must be challenged.

# 3. Core Messaging Principles

## 3.1 Clarity Over Cleverness

Headlines must explain value clearly.

Prefer:

> Grow Your Amazon & eBay Store With Expert Marketplace Management

Avoid vague headlines such as:

> Unlock Your Digital Potential

The visitor must understand the service without needing to interpret marketing language.

## 3.2 Outcome-Oriented Messaging

Service descriptions should explain the business outcome, not only the task.

Weak:

> We create product listings.

Strong:

> We create structured, search-focused product listings designed to improve discoverability, clarity, and buyer confidence.

## 3.3 Never Overpromise

Do not promise guaranteed sales, guaranteed rankings, guaranteed profits, guaranteed account approval, or guaranteed claim outcomes.

Avoid phrases such as:

- Guaranteed profit.
- Guaranteed sales.
- #1 ranking guaranteed.
- Guaranteed account recovery.
- Guaranteed A-to-Z claim victory.
- Instant store growth.

Use qualified, evidence-based language instead:

- Designed to improve.
- Built to support.
- Focused on.
- Data-informed.
- Strategy-driven.
- Designed around marketplace best practices.

## 3.4 Claims Require Evidence

Any public numerical claim must be supported by client-provided evidence.

Examples include:

- Revenue.
- Profit.
- Number of managed stores.
- Number of clients.
- Growth percentage.
- Order count.
- Conversion rate.
- Years of experience.

If evidence is unavailable, use non-numeric positioning instead of inventing statistics.

## 3.5 Explain Marketplace Terms

Technical or marketplace-specific language should be understandable to ordinary sellers.

Examples:

- Use **Product Hunting & Research** instead of only “Hunting”.
- Use **Listing Creation & Optimization** instead of only “Listing”.
- Use **Order Management** instead of only “Order”.
- Use **Customer Support** instead of only “Customer Care”.
- Use **Amazon A-to-Z Claim Support** instead of only “A to Z”.

## 7.1 Primary Conversion

**Free Consultation / Store Assessment**

## 7.2 Secondary Conversions

- Explore services.
- View case studies.
- Contact via WhatsApp where appropriate.

# 8. Lead Form Principles

The consultation form should collect enough information to qualify a lead without becoming unnecessarily long.

Recommended fields:

- Name.
- Email.
- WhatsApp/phone.
- Marketplace.
- Store status.
- Approximate revenue range, when relevant.
- Required service.
- Message.

Marketplace options:

- Amazon.
- eBay.
- Both.

Store-status options:

- New store.
- Existing store.
- Sales need improvement.
- Need complete management.

The form must clearly explain what happens after submission.

Example:

> We review your requirements and contact you with the most suitable next step.

Do not imply a consultation is free unless it actually is.

# 9. Trust and Credibility Principles

Trust is a primary design objective.

The website should use evidence wherever available:

- Verified testimonials.
- Real case studies.
- Real team information.
- Real process explanations.
- Real screenshots where legally and commercially permitted.
- Verified statistics.
- Clear contact information.
- Clear service boundaries.

Avoid fabricated:

- Testimonials.
- Reviews.
- Client logos.
- Revenue numbers.
- Awards.
- Certifications.
- Team members.
- Years of experience.

Place trust proof near decision points rather than only in the footer.

---

# 10. Case Study Principles

Case studies should tell a business story instead of showing only impressive numbers.

Recommended structure:

### Challenge

What was happening before the engagement?

### Strategy

What actions were taken?

### Execution

What marketplace operations or optimizations were performed?

### Result

What changed?

### Evidence

Where appropriate, show screenshots, charts, or documented metrics.

Every result must identify relevant context such as timeframe when available.

Never present an isolated metric without enough context to avoid misleading interpretation.

# 11. New Store / Zero-Rating Positioning

New-store support can be a major differentiator.

The website may present a journey such as:

**New Store → Product Strategy → Optimized Listings → First Orders → Positive Feedback → Sustainable Growth**

Do not imply that positive feedback or sales can be guaranteed.

Use wording such as:

> We help new sellers build a stronger foundation for their first marketplace orders and long-term store growth.

Marketplace work must be described as policy-conscious.

- Manipulative behavior.
- Fake reviews.
- Fake engagement.
- Circumvention of marketplace rules.
- Fraudulent claims.
- Misleading product information.
- Unauthorized account access.
- Prohibited listing practices.

# 17. Visual Design Constitution

## 17.1 Overall Style

The design should combine:

- Premium SaaS.
- Modern e-commerce.
- Professional consulting.
- Data visualization.

Visual inspiration should be conceptual, not copied from another brand.

## 17.2 Color System

Preferred direction:

- Deep navy or charcoal as a strong foundation.
- White/light neutral backgrounds for content sections.
- A restrained orange or warm accent for important marketplace/e-commerce highlights.
- Neutral grays for secondary information.

The final palette must pass accessibility contrast checks.

Do not use many unrelated accent colors.

## 17.3 Typography

Recommended families:

- Inter.
- Manrope.
- Plus Jakarta Sans.

Typography must feel modern, legible, and professional.

Use a clear hierarchy:

- Display/H1.
- H2.
- H3.
- Body.
- Small/meta.

Avoid excessively small body text.

## 17.4 Spacing

Use a consistent spacing system.

Prefer generous whitespace over crowded interfaces.

## 17.5 Cards

Cards should exist for a reason.

Use cards for:

- Services.
- Metrics.
- Case studies.
- Features.
- Process steps.

Avoid turning every paragraph into a separate card.

Visuals should reinforce the business story.

- Marketplace dashboards.

- Listing mockups.
- Product cards.

---

# 19. Motion and Animation Principles

Motion should improve comprehension and polish, not distract.

Use:

- Subtle entrance transitions.
- Scroll reveal.
- Small hover interactions.
- Counter animations for verified metrics.
- Graph animation.
- Smooth section transitions.

Avoid:

- Excessive parallax.
- Large spinning elements.
- Constant floating effects.
- Long blocking animations.
- Motion on every element.

All important information must remain usable with reduced motion preferences.

---

# 20. Responsive Design Constitution

The website must be designed mobile-first while still feeling premium on desktop.

Required breakpoints should support at minimum:

- Mobile.
- Tablet.
- Desktop.
- Large desktop.

Mobile must not feel like a compressed desktop page.

Specific mobile requirements:

- Easy-to-tap buttons.
- Readable headings.
- No horizontal overflow.
- Simplified navigation.
- Appropriate spacing.
- Forms optimized for mobile.
- Sticky or accessible consultation CTA where appropriate.

---

# 21. Accessibility Constitution

Accessibility is a requirement, not an optional enhancement.

The implementation should target WCAG-aligned good practices.

Requirements include:

- Semantic HTML.
- Keyboard navigability.
- Visible focus states.
- Accessible form labels.
- Useful alt text.
- Sufficient color contrast.
- Logical heading hierarchy.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Accessible buttons and links.
- No information conveyed only by color.

Do not sacrifice accessibility for visual effects.

# 27. Privacy and Data Principles

Forms and analytics must be implemented responsibly.

Do not collect unnecessary personal information.

The site must clearly explain relevant data collection practices.

Any analytics, tracking, marketing tags, or CRM integrations must be implemented according to the applicable legal and platform requirements.

Contact forms should have validation and spam protection.

Never expose submitted form data publicly.

---

# 28. Security Constitution

The website must follow secure implementation practices.

Requirements include:

- Environment variables for secrets.
- No API keys in client-side source.
- Server-side validation where appropriate.
- Input sanitization/validation.
- Rate limiting for public endpoints when needed.
- Spam protection.
- Secure headers where appropriate.
- Proper authentication for administrative areas.
- Dependency updates and vulnerability checks.

Do not place sensitive credentials inside repository files.

---

# 29. Performance Constitution

Performance is a business requirement because slow pages reduce trust and conversion.

The implementation should prioritize:

- Fast initial render.
- Optimized images.
- Responsive image sizing.
- Lazy loading where appropriate.
- Minimal client-side JavaScript.
- Component-level code splitting where appropriate.
- Efficient font loading.
- Avoidance of unnecessary third-party scripts.

Animations must not significantly damage performance.

Aim for strong Core Web Vitals and excellent real-world mobile performance.

---

# 30. Next.js Implementation Principles

The preferred architecture should be modern Next.js with clear separation of server and client responsibilities.

Use server-rendered content wherever interactivity does not require a client component.

Use client components only where necessary, such as:

- Interactive forms.
- Animations.
- Carousels.
- Dynamic client-side controls.
- UI requiring browser APIs.

Prefer reusable components for:

- Buttons.
- Cards.
- Sections.
- Typography.
- Navigation.
- Forms.
- Metrics.
- Case-study layouts.

Do not build large monolithic page components.

# 34. Interaction Rules

Buttons must look clickable.

Links must behave consistently.

Hover states must not be the only way to communicate interaction.

Forms must provide:

- Clear labels.
- Validation.
- Error messages.
- Loading state.
- Success state.

Never silently fail after form submission.

---

# 35. Error and Empty States

The website must handle:

- Form errors.
- Failed submissions.
- Missing content.
- Image loading failures.
- Empty case-study lists in future CMS states.
- Network failures where applicable.

Errors should be understandable to ordinary users.

Do not display raw technical stack traces.

---

# 36. Accessibility and Internationalization Readiness

Even if the first release uses one language, the content system should not make future localization unnecessarily difficult.

Avoid hardcoding text into complex graphical assets when plain HTML text would work.

Do not assume English is the only future language.

---

# 37. Analytics and Measurement Constitution

The site should measure business outcomes, not only traffic.

Recommended events may include:

- Consultation CTA click.
- Consultation form start.
- Consultation form submission.
- WhatsApp click.
- Email click.
- Phone click.
- Case study engagement.
- Service page engagement.

The exact analytics stack will be defined in the implementation specification.

# 38. A/B Testing Principles

When testing copy or layouts, test one meaningful hypothesis at a time.

Potential hypotheses:

- Consultation CTA wording.
- Hero headline.
- Form length.
- Case-study placement.
- Service ordering.

Do not randomly change multiple variables and then claim a causal result.

---

# 39. Mobile Conversion Rules

On mobile:

- Keep the primary CTA easy to access.
- Make forms short enough to complete comfortably.
- Avoid huge decorative graphics above important content.
- Keep key proof near service explanations.
- Use readable metric cards.
- Avoid horizontal tables where cards or stacked content would be better.

---

# 40. Trust-Building Content Order

When possible, the website should present:

**Claim → Explanation → Evidence → CTA**

Example:

> We optimize marketplace listings.

Then explain the optimization process.

Then show a real before/after example.

Then invite the user to request an assessment.

This structure is preferable to making a claim with no evidence.

# 41. Anti-Pattern Constitution

The website must avoid the following:

## Generic Agency Hero

No vague “we transform businesses” messaging.

## Service Dump

Do not place twenty services on the page without hierarchy.

## Fake Stats

Never invent client numbers or revenue figures.

## Fake Testimonials

Never create testimonial text and present it as real.

## Fake Team

Never create imaginary team members.

## Overloaded Design

Do not use too many cards, gradients, animations, or badges.

## Stock Photo Dependency

Do not fill the website with unrelated stock photos.

## Excessive Buzzwords

Avoid strings such as:

> Cutting-edge, revolutionary, next-generation, synergistic, disruptive.

## Dark Pattern CTAs

Never manipulate users into submissions.

## Forced Popups

Avoid intrusive popups that block users before they understand the service.

## SEO Spam

Do not create dozens of near-duplicate pages solely to capture keywords.

# 47. Decision-Making Hierarchy

When two decisions conflict, follow this priority order:

1. Truthfulness.
2. User clarity.
3. Business conversion.
4. Accessibility.
5. Performance.
6. SEO quality.
7. Visual polish.
8. Novelty.

A visually impressive element must never override truthfulness, usability, or performance.
